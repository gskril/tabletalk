The direction.

Make Tabletalk feel like an invitation. Every saved place is somewhere you might take a friend; every list has a little story behind it.

01

Put the company next to the food

Show a good photograph and a short reason to go together. Friends should be visible as people with opinions, not as social metrics.

02

Keep the personal touches few

A small caption or a lightly offset note creates warmth. Use it once in a view, with plenty of clean structure around it.

03

Build around sharing

Collections carry an occasion and an author. Public lists should be easy to read before signing in and easy to keep afterward.

A small,
coherent system.

Keep the editorial foundation of The Regular. Tune the typography, color, and photography together so the warmth carries across the whole application.

Typography and identity

Body, navigation, controls

Wordmark use. Newsreader 400 with modest tightening. Keep it on a solid, light surface. Minimum width 112 px and one lowercase “o” of clear space. Personal notes never overlap the wordmark.

Use porcelain for the page and apricot linen for a welcoming header or a note. Terracotta is an action color, not a background for every card. Keep text cocoa or darker.

Type hierarchyNewsreader 400 with occasional italic for 48–72 px headlines and 28–32 px titles. DM Sans handles all body text and metadata. Use the serif, not a handwriting font, for personal notes.
Layout and shape7 px corners, 24–32 px gaps, and an occasional warm note panel. Keep slight rotation confined to a single photo caption; forms and notebook rows remain straight and orderly.
PhotographyShared plates, people in restaurants, and the evidence of a meal in progress. Crop close enough to feel present. Keep portraits natural and give authors a real name.
Comfort is usability, tooKeep controls at least 44 px tall, use visible focus states, and meet AA text contrast. Photographs need a preview size, lazy loading below the fold, and a stable aspect ratio.

A familiar
voice.

Generous, familiar, and a little conversational. Talk about who to take and what to order. Leave room for the person’s own words.

Feels right
“A few places for the next time we’re all free.”

Something useful you could say to a friend.

Feels wrong
“Find your foodie tribe and level up your social dining.”

Generic promises distract from the actual places and people.

Across
the application.

Apply the identity to the features already working first. Then make it easier to share public lists and follow friends’ recommendations. Collaborative editing can come next.

01

Discover

A dish to get your attention, then a friend’s note or list that makes it relevant. Keep the next action simple.

02

Restaurant pages

Pair food and room photographs with full reviews. A friend’s useful note matters more than a large anonymous score.

03

Notebook and profiles

Make collections feel personal through titles, captions, and authorship. Public profiles show visited places and reviews in the same warm, readable system.

Best fit. The strongest expression of eating together. Pick this if you want the app to feel especially personal, with lists and friends’ notes at its heart.

Keep an eye on. Keep the styling disciplined. Too many tilted notes or warm panels would make it feel like a scrapbook instead of a useful restaurant app.

Across all three: Blackbird sign-in, reviews gated by verified visits, guest-readable public lists, and private visit dates. The social warmth comes from the presentation and the people, with the same clear account boundaries.
