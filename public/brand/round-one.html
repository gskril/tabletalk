The brief.

Take inspiration from Blackbird’s confidence, hospitality, and sense of place. Give Tabletalk a younger, more personal point of view: posh enough for a special evening, relaxed enough for a Saturday lunch. Keep the name for this exploration.

What changes.

Less decorative badge clutter and generic card framing. More precise typography, stronger photography, useful hierarchy, and recommendations with a person behind them. Each direction includes an app preview, a practical visual system, voice examples, and a product roadmap.

Three ways to set the table.
Direction Best at Social expression Watch out for
01 · The Regular Daily discovery and personal taste Friends’ notes and considered lists Too much serif can slow dense screens
02 · Table for Friends Shared lists and group decisions Collaborators, shortlists, and plans Too much color makes it feel juvenile
03 · After Hours Occasions and evening discovery Plans for a whole night together Atmosphere must not hide useful details

My starting point.

The Regular is the best fit for the product today. It brings the polish you’re looking for while leaving room for a lively social layer. I’d take its visual identity and pair it with the shared-list ideas in Table for Friends. Choose After Hours if planning nights out should become the product’s main focus.
