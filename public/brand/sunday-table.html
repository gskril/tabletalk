The direction.

Make the application feel like taking a seat. Food leads, a friend gives it context, and the next action is easy to find.

01

Start with the food

Use one large, appetizing photograph, with supporting restaurant images beneath. Let daylight, ceramics, and real ingredients set the mood.

02

Make recommendations personal

Write “On Alex’s lunch list” rather than “Trending near you.” Use the person’s reason for going, not decorative popularity markers.

03

Keep it easygoing

Wide margins, short descriptions, and comfortable type sizes. Warmth should make the page easier to use, not more embellished.

A small,
coherent system.

Keep the editorial foundation of The Regular. Tune the typography, color, and photography together so the warmth carries across the whole application.

Typography and identity

Body, navigation, controls

Wordmark use. Newsreader 400, gently tightened. Keep a full lowercase “o” of clear space on each side. Minimum width 112 px in navigation; no crest, mascot, or stamp around it.

Color

Use warm paper for the reading surface, butter for one welcoming panel, and olive for actions and the wordmark. Keep food colors natural.

Type hierarchyNewsreader 400 for 48–68 px headlines and 28–32 px restaurant names; DM Sans 400/500 for 16 px body and 14 px controls. Let body copy stay plain.
Layout and shape4 px corners, a 1 px warm-gray rule, and 24–32 px space between sections. Give the main food photograph almost half the first screen. On mobile, copy comes before the photograph.
PhotographyTables in daylight, dishes for sharing, and rooms with signs of life. Avoid cold studio styling or strong color filters. Use 4:3 and landscape crops.
Comfort is usability, tooKeep controls at least 44 px tall, use visible focus states, and meet AA text contrast. Photographs need a preview size, lazy loading below the fold, and a stable aspect ratio.

A familiar
voice.

Warm, specific, unhurried. Mention the dish, the occasion, or the company. The copy should sound like someone who has actually been.

Feels right
“Good for a late lunch. Better if nobody has plans afterward.”

Something useful you could say to a friend.

Feels wrong
“Your exclusive passport to unforgettable culinary journeys.”

Generic promises distract from the actual places and people.

Across
the application.

Apply the identity to the features already working first. Then make it easier to share public lists and follow friends’ recommendations. Collaborative editing can come next.

01

Discover

One generous food image, followed by a small set of friends’ recommendations. Keep search and filters within easy reach.

02

Restaurant pages

The restaurant name and photograph lead. Show useful details, actual reviews, and people who have been, with clear visit verification.

03

Notebook and profiles

Use photo rows and personal notes. Keep Saved places, Been there, and Lists distinct; show public visited places without private dates.

Best fit. The most comforting variation, and my recommendation for the next round. It is an easy place to spend time and still feels considered.

Keep an eye on. Keep the olive deep and the typography crisp. Too many beige panels would make the whole application feel flat.

Across all three: Blackbird sign-in, reviews gated by verified visits, guest-readable public lists, and private visit dates. The social warmth comes from the presentation and the people, with the same clear account boundaries.
