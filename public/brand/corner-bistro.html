The direction.

Treat the restaurant app like a very useful neighborhood dining guide. Give every place a name, a room, and a person who recommends it.

01

Borrow from a good menu

Use a strong masthead, aligned columns, and fine rules. Keep the restaurant information readable and consistent across every card.

02

Give each place its due

Three equal restaurant photographs make comparison easy. Lead with names and neighborhoods, then let a friend’s note add the personality.

03

Be familiar, not nostalgic

A hint of restaurant stationery is enough. Skip faux paper textures, retro badges, and illustrated cutlery.

A small,
coherent system.

Keep the editorial foundation of The Regular. Tune the typography, color, and photography together so the warmth carries across the whole application.

Body, navigation, controls

Wordmark use. Newsreader 500, centered when there is room. Use a double rule as a layout detail rather than a container around the logo. Minimum width 112 px, with one lowercase “o” of clear space.

Keep cream as the main surface. Use house wine for the identity, primary action, and a few fine rules. Blush linen should appear sparingly in supporting sections.

Type hierarchyNewsreader 500 for the wordmark, 400 for headlines and names. Use italic for one short phrase per view. DM Sans 400/500 for 16 px body and 14 px navigation.
Layout and shape1 px corners, 3 px double rules at major divisions, and consistent card baselines. Use a three-column restaurant layout on desktop and generous photo rows on mobile.
PhotographyRooms, dishes, and the table itself. Match image heights without imposing one photographic mood on every restaurant. Each venue should keep its character.
Comfort is usability, tooKeep controls at least 44 px tall, use visible focus states, and meet AA text contrast. Photographs need a preview size, lazy loading below the fold, and a stable aspect ratio.

A familiar
voice.

Confident and neighborly. A short opinion can do more than a long description. Use specific observations and occasional dry humor.

Feels right
“The place to suggest when nobody wants to make a decision.”

Something useful you could say to a friend.

Feels wrong
“Curated dining experiences for discerning tastemakers.”

Generic promises distract from the actual places and people.

Across
the application.

Apply the identity to the features already working first. Then make it easier to share public lists and follow friends’ recommendations. Collaborative editing can come next.

01

Discover

A strong masthead and a restrained restaurant grid. Put friends’ context directly under each place, rather than in separate badges.

02

Restaurant pages

A large name, a useful photo gallery, and a compact “at a glance” information row. Make review writing calm and straightforward.

03

Notebook and profiles

Keep list titles editorial, but use clear aligned rows for saved places and visits. Let the user’s reviews supply the personality.

Best fit. The strongest identity and the clearest continuation of The Regular. Pick this if you want to retain more of the original’s polish and structure.

Keep an eye on. Use the menu references lightly. Too many rules or centered headings can turn a useful app into a restaurant website.

Across all three: Blackbird sign-in, reviews gated by verified visits, guest-readable public lists, and private visit dates. The social warmth comes from the presentation and the people, with the same clear account boundaries.
