The idea.

A city guide for the hours you look forward to. Give restaurant discovery the feeling of an evening unfolding, while keeping the practical details one tap away.

01

Let the room set the mood

Give photography space. Put a short, striking headline next to it and keep operational details clear underneath.

02

Luxury without a velvet rope

Be generous, not exclusive. People should feel invited to make plans, with useful price and location information always visible.

03

A whole evening in view

Lists can connect a dinner spot to the next stop. Show the story of a night, without pretending an itinerary is a confirmed booking.

65% night and charcoal · 25% photography · 10% ivory and orchid. Orchid highlights the next action. Keep form text ivory on a solid dark surface.

Type hierarchyDisplay headlines 64–96 px desktop, 48–60 px mobile. Use Bodoni only above 28 px so fine strokes hold up. Body 16–18 px. Labels 14 px.
Spacing and shapeBroad full-width photography with compact information below. 32–40 px desktop gutters; 20–24 px mobile. 2 px corners, thin dividers, no heavy card shadows.
PhotographyWarm interiors, evening tables, and people in the room. Use a dark overlay only behind text; preserve a useful, unfiltered restaurant image in the detail gallery.
Motion200 ms image and opacity transitions. No autoplay video in the app. Respect reduced motion and keep every action responsive.

✓ Visited with Blackbird

Reviews come from verified visits. A save is simply a place you want to remember.

Rules for every screen

  • Use atmospheric imagery at discovery and detail level. Saved lists and forms use simple, legible rows.
  • Show price, neighborhood, and dietary notes in a solid-color area rather than over a photograph.
  • Plans have explicit states: an idea, agreed with friends, or booked externally. Never imply a reservation exists.
  • Reviews stay grounded in actual Blackbird visits. No premium styling should obscure what is verified.
  • Use visible labels, keyboard focus, and at least 44 px touch targets. Meet WCAG AA contrast for interface text; never communicate state with color alone.

How it
sounds.

Understated, intimate, and concrete. Invite people into a good evening. Avoid concierge language, insider posturing, and promises of special access.

Say something like
“Dinner first. We’ll see where the night goes.”

Specific, useful, and comfortable to say out loud.

Leave this behind
“Exclusive access to the city’s most elite dining experiences.”

The people and the places already make it interesting.

Where it
goes next.

First, apply the direction to the working product. Then build the social features that fit it. Stages two and three are proposals, not features already available.

01 / Refine what exists

A useful evening guide

  • DiscoverStrong photography and clear filters for neighborhood, price, and occasion.
  • RestaurantA cinematic lead, followed by reviews, practical details, and genuine public lists.
  • Notebook + profileCompact, readable saved places and visit history; dark mode must work beyond the hero.
02 / Social next

Put a night together

  • Itinerary listsOrder a few restaurants or bars into a plan with optional times and notes.
  • Share with friendsA guest-readable plan, with a separate invite to collaborate.
  • Following activityRecent nights and recommendations from people you know, without revealing private check-in dates.
03 / Extend carefully

More useful after six

  • Map planningCompare walking distance between saved stops, using documented map data.
  • Practical remindersOpt-in reminders tied to plans, not promotional notifications.
  • Reservation linksLink to each venue’s existing booking page. In-app availability requires a supported partner integration.
The tradeoff. Best for occasion-led discovery and dinner plans. It has the strongest mood, but it will need careful contrast and a light reading mode if notebook use becomes frequent. It is a larger move away from a daily social journal.
